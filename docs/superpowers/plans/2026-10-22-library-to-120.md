For each group of roughly twelve, avoid consecutive stories with the same century, place, family pattern, work, emotional arc or ending. First establish the documented constraints of the specific historical time and place. Then **invent a full, coherent life within them** and choose one episode worth reading as a short novel. Interest need not mean a happy or comic plot: devotion, loneliness, grief, conflict, surprise and a costly choice can all hold attention. A large family's eldest child who spends years caring for siblings and later faces a lonely old age is one possible life, not a template or required outcome. Do not turn a story into a tour of historical facts or force a family event into the chronology merely to fill a category. A plausible, distinctive life takes priority over hitting a cell in either table exactly.

For every new story, summarize the protagonist's **whole life** in 01 through livelihood, relationships and changes across years. Treat personality as one part of the profile. Do not define an entire life by the moral, flaw or conflict of the single event chosen for 02, and do not give away that event's conclusion in 01. Confirm the life summary still describes the person when the featured episode is hidden. Apply this review to the 45th pilot before promotion and to all later manuscripts.
